Pattern (1)

Why aesthetic intuition matters in technical work, and what hardware design teaches us about simplicity.

When we talk about engineering, we often talk in terms of throughput, latency, clock speeds, and efficiency metrics. But some of the best systems ever built weren't just fast; they had a distinct sense of taste behind them.

The Unseen Aesthetics

Good taste in engineering is knowing what to leave out. It is the restraint to avoid unnecessary complexity when a simpler datapath or cleaner abstraction gets the job done reliably.

Whether you are writing C code for a RISC-V microcontroller or designing an interface, the principles remain remarkably similar: remove friction, respect the constraints, and build something that feels natural to use.
